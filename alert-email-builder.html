Alert Email Builder

A way for model owners to get notified automatically when something needs their attention, instead of relying on someone remembering to check.

Role
Product owner, end to end
Context
Corridor Platforms
Built
Condition-based triggers · Variable-aware email editor · Severity levels

4 min read

The problem

A dashboard only helps if someone actually looks at it. Reviews go quietly overdue because nobody is checking every model every day, and nobody finds out until it matters.

The approach

Let an owner define a condition, like a review date going overdue, and have the platform send the email automatically, with the severity and the exact wording under their control.

How we judged it

Could someone build a working alert, with a correctly personalised email, without writing any code and without a typo breaking it silently?

Real client details and data are confidential. The prototype below uses a fictional bank, Northwind Decisions, and made-up numbers.

Framing

The email editor was the real product

The trigger logic (if this condition is true, send this email) is the straightforward half. The part that took real thought was the email body itself. An alert is only useful if the person reading it immediately understands what's wrong and with which model, so the body needed to pull in real values: the model's name, who owns it, why it fired.

That meant variables, and variables meant a new way to get it wrong. Someone typing {model_nam} instead of {model_name} would send a broken email to a real inbox with no warning. So the editor validates every variable before it lets you save. Catching that typo before send mattered more than any amount of help text would have.

Outcome

Built, and quietly doing its job

Alerts ship on top of the monitoring dashboard, so a review date going overdue or a condition being met sends the right email automatically instead of waiting for someone to notice. The walkthrough above is a recreation of the real editor, rebuilt against a fictional bank so I could show it publicly.

What I'd take further

  • Whether email is still the right channel. It works, but the people who own these models mostly live in Slack or Teams day to day. An email can still become one more thing to skim past.
  • How much customisation actually gets used. Most alerts end up close to a handful of templates in practice. Worth checking whether the fully open editor is earning its own complexity.

Takeaway

Validation is a design decision, not a safety net

It would have been easy to treat the typo-catching as a small technical detail bolted on at the end. It wasn't. Deciding exactly when to block someone (on save, not after) and exactly what counts as broken (an unknown variable, not just a typo anywhere) is what made the feature trustworthy enough for someone to actually rely on it.
