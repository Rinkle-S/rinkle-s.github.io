Insights to Presentation

A faster path from a portfolio report to a deck someone can actually present, without starting from a blank slide.

Role
Product owner, end to end
Context
Corridor Platforms
Built
AI-generated insights · Slide editor · Rerun to flag stale insights

4 min read

The problem

Portfolio reviews end in a deck. Getting from "here's what changed this month" to something a committee can sit through usually means a few hours of someone copying numbers into slides by hand.

The approach

Generate an insight on a report, turn it into a draft presentation, edit it like a normal slide, and present straight from the same screen. Rerunning the analysis flags anything that's gone stale.

How we judged it

Is the resulting deck something someone would actually present as is, and does rerunning genuinely catch numbers that have moved on?

Real client details and data are confidential. The prototype below uses a fictional bank, Northwind Decisions, and its AI text is scripted rather than live.

Framing

Generating the slide is the easy half

Turning a report into a first-draft slide is the part that looks impressive in a demo. It's also the part that was straightforward to get working. The harder, less flashy problem showed up after: once that slide exists, it starts going out of date the moment the underlying report changes, and nothing told anyone that had happened.

So the feature isn't really "generate a slide." It's "generate a slide, and tell me the moment it stops being true." Rerun became the part I cared about most, not the generation itself.

Outcome

Built, with the AI text scripted for this walkthrough

The flow (generate, edit, present, rerun) is real. The AI-generated text in the walkthrough above is scripted rather than live, so I could show it publicly without a real portfolio report behind it.

What I'd take further

  • Whether editing a draft is actually faster than starting fresh. A generated slide that still needs heavy editing might not save anyone real time. Worth timing both ways rather than assuming.
  • How noisy the rerun flag gets. If nearly every slide gets flagged stale after a routine data refresh, people will learn to ignore it, which defeats the point.

Takeaway

Staleness, not generation, was the insight

Everyone wants the AI-writes-my-slide feature. Fewer people ask what happens to that slide a week later, after the report underneath it has moved. That gap, a deck that quietly stops being true, is the more interesting product problem, and it's the one this feature actually solves.
